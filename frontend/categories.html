01 / Medicine supply

🔐 Legal Access & Control

Supply rules depend on the product and local law. Ask a qualified pharmacist about the requirements for a specific medicine.

01
Non-prescription where permitted

Over-the-Counter Medicines

Common Uses

Some products are used for minor pain, fever, acidity or allergy symptoms, depending on the ingredient.

Potential Benefits

May provide convenient relief for suitable minor symptoms when used as directed.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Read the label, check active ingredients and avoid taking duplicate products with the same ingredient.

02
Professional Guidance

Pharmacist-Guided Medicines

Common Uses

The appropriate product depends on symptoms, health history and the medicine's local supply rules.

Potential Benefits

A pharmacist can help explain directions, precautions and possible interactions.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Tell the pharmacist about allergies, pregnancy, ongoing conditions and other medicines you take.

03
Prescription Required

Prescription Medicines

Common Uses

Examples include many antibiotics, insulin products and medicines for blood pressure.

Potential Benefits

May help treat or manage specific conditions with appropriate medical supervision.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Use only as prescribed. Do not share medicines or change the dose without professional advice.

04
Strict Legal Controls

Controlled Medicines

Common Uses

Some opioid pain medicines, sedatives and stimulants are used for specific medical needs.

Potential Benefits

Can have important treatment benefits when prescribed and carefully monitored.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Dependence, overdose and interactions can occur. Follow prescription, storage and disposal instructions.

02 / Purpose of treatment

🩺 Therapeutic Categories

Medicines are grouped here by their general treatment purpose. The exact use depends on the individual product and patient.

01
Pain Management

Analgesics — Pain Relief

Common Uses

Relief of certain headaches, muscle pain and other painful conditions.

Potential Benefits

May reduce discomfort and help with everyday activities.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Different pain medicines have different risks. Follow the label and avoid exceeding the recommended dose.

02
Infection Treatment

Antibiotics & Antimicrobials

Common Uses

Antibiotics treat susceptible bacterial infections when prescribed appropriately.

Potential Benefits

An appropriate antibiotic can help treat a bacterial infection.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Antibiotics do not treat viral colds or flu. Do not self-medicate or share a prescription.

03
Allergy Relief

Antihistamines

Common Uses

Relief of selected allergy symptoms such as sneezing, itching and a runny nose.

Potential Benefits

May reduce discomfort associated with allergic symptoms.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Some cause drowsiness. Check the product warning before driving or combining with alcohol.

04
Digestive Health

Antacids & GI Medicines

Common Uses

Selected products help relieve heartburn, acidity and certain digestive symptoms.

Potential Benefits

May reduce discomfort related to stomach acid, depending on the product.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Some interact with other medicines. Get advice if symptoms persist or are severe.

05
Heart & Blood Pressure

Cardiovascular Medicines

Common Uses

Management of high blood pressure and selected heart or circulation conditions.

Potential Benefits

Appropriate treatment may control blood pressure and reduce certain health risks.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Follow monitoring advice. Do not stop prescribed treatment without medical guidance.

06
Temperature Relief

Antipyretics — Fever Relief

Common Uses

Help reduce fever and may ease related discomfort.

Potential Benefits

May improve comfort while the cause of the fever is addressed.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Fever has many causes. Seek medical care for severe symptoms or persistent fever.

07
Blood Clot Management

Anticoagulants

Common Uses

Prevent or treat certain harmful blood clots under medical supervision.

Potential Benefits

Can reduce specific clot-related complications when appropriately used.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Bleeding is an important risk. Report unusual bleeding promptly and follow monitoring advice.

03 / Medicine formats

💊 Dosage Forms

A dosage form is the physical form in which a medicine is prepared. Instructions vary by product.

01
Oral Solid Forms

Tablets, Capsules & Chewables

Common Uses

Oral medicines for many different conditions, depending on the active ingredient.

Potential Benefits

Often convenient to carry, store and take according to directions.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Do not crush, split or open a medicine unless its instructions say it is safe.

02
Oral Liquid Forms

Syrups & Suspensions

Common Uses

Liquid medicines designed to be taken by mouth.

Potential Benefits

May be easier for people who have difficulty swallowing tablets.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Shake a suspension if directed and measure with the correct dosing device.

03
Topical Forms

Creams, Ointments & Gels

Common Uses

Applied to the skin for selected skin conditions or specific purposes.

Potential Benefits

Some formulations deliver treatment directly to the affected area.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Follow the label and avoid eyes or sensitive areas unless the product is intended for them.

04
Inhaled / Nasal Forms

Inhalers & Sprays

Common Uses

Some inhalers deliver medicine to the airways; nasal sprays are designed for the nose.

Potential Benefits

The delivery method can target a particular area of the body.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Correct technique matters. Follow instructions and ask a healthcare professional for help.

05
Parenteral Forms

Injectable Medicines

Common Uses

Medicines given by injection when that route is medically appropriate.

Potential Benefits

May be useful when a medicine needs to be given by a non-oral route.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Injections require appropriate training, sterile equipment and professional guidance.

06
Transdermal Forms

Patches

Common Uses

Some patches deliver a specific medicine through the skin over a period of time.

Potential Benefits

Selected products can provide a steady delivery method.

⚠️ Safety Tips

Follow application and disposal instructions. Do not cut a patch unless specifically permitted.

Important Medicine Safety Notice

This page is for educational purposes only and is not a substitute for professional medical advice. Legal supply classifications vary by location and product. Use medicines according to their labels and advice from a qualified healthcare professional. Do not self-medicate with antibiotics, prescription medicines or controlled medicines. Seek urgent medical care in an emergency.
